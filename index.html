Sunlight, contact with Earth and safe ground for every site near the Moon’s south pole, worked out from NASA data.

At the south pole, the Sun never rises high.

It circles the horizon a degree or two up. Every hill throws a shadow tens of kilometres long, and the shadows turn with the Sun.

A hill far away can hide it for days.

Some crater floors have not seen sunlight for billions of years. They are among the coldest places known, and may hold water ice.

Earth hangs low on the horizon.

When it sinks behind a ridge, a lander cannot call home. Where you land decides when you have power and when you can talk.
